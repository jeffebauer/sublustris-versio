## Summary

The ten findings most worth your time, most valuable first. Tags: area; **measured** (run tonight), **estimated** (with assumptions) or **literature**.

1. **About 5 KB of flash, with no change to the sound** *(flash; measured sizes, needs the module)*. Release is 76 B under budget today, which blocks every fix below. Two firmware switches take it to **107,236 B, 5.4 KB under**:
   - `LEAN=1` (−2,144 B): compiler choices and an unused I²C path;
   - `AUDIO_DIRECT=1` (−3,240 B): the audio callback without libDaisy's general-purpose layer.

   The morning's profile build proves LEAN on the module (two HASH lines that must match); one rack boot proves AUDIO_DIRECT.
2. **The rumble at endless is found and fixed** *(sound; measured)*. The floor governor nudges the level up and down to hold it, but its down nudges take highs and its up nudges add lows. Over minutes an endless tail or a freeze drains its sparkle (−17 to −46 dB above 2 kHz in 10 minutes) and piles up sub-bass. The fix makes the nudge a plain volume change, lets the low tank hold its own level, and adds a 5 Hz DC blocker on the way in. Every band holds within ±1 dB over 10 minutes. +568 B, so it needs item 1. **Page `review2_endless`.**
3. **DC coming in ruins an endless tail** *(robustness; measured, re-checked by me)*. With 0.02 of DC under the music, an endless PLATE's tail ends up a held offset with the music 28 dB down. On the module the codec's high-pass probably catches steady DC; the **Plugin has nothing**. The 5 Hz blocker in item 2 fixes it (−0.2 dB at 20-40 Hz).
4. **MASSIVE has a hole in its low mids at SIZE noon, the default** *(sound; measured)*. Its two tanks ring alike near their 300 Hz crossover and cancel: −3 to −6.7 dB at 200-400 Hz, also at SIZE 0.70, 0.75 and 1.0, and the endless level sits up to 3.5 dB under its floor there. Fix: the low tank's lines 6 % shorter, +24 B. Draft pair `review2_hole_draft`; it needs a page.
5. **MASSIVE's "bump" is really a cliff at 3 o'clock** *(sound; measured, re-checked by me)*. The floor governor switches on within about 3° of the pot, a 2.5-4.6 dB step when played normally or hot. A gentle ramp from 0.70 (and the same for PLATE) makes it a glide, with endless and everything at or below 0.70 unchanged to the bit. +64 B. **Page `review2_bump`.**
6. **MASSIVE's CPU: about 4.5 more points, and one corner nobody has timed** *(CPU; estimated, part measured)*. Four exact savings plus two maths shortcuts far below hearing (`ABSORB_EXP`, `SHIMMER_POLY`) should take the worst lap line from ~95 % to ~90 %. **Unfreezing after SIZE moved while frozen** is estimated at 96-103 %, the likeliest late block left. The profile build times both.
7. **The Plugin at 96 kHz sounds much brighter than the module** *(Plugin; measured, re-checked by me)*. HALL and MASSIVE's half-rate tank no longer rolls off above 12 kHz: 50-63 dB more at 14-20 kHz. At 44.1 kHz they lose 2-4 dB at 11 kHz. Worth checking Ableton's sample rate.
8. **CV: SIZE and DEPTH follow only slow CV, and short triggers can miss the gate** *(controls; measured with the firmware's knob path ported to the desktop)*. SIZE ignores anything faster than ~6 % of its travel a second (an LFO holds the room, then it jumps when the LFO stops). Triggers under ~1 ms can slip between gate reads. PLATE's SIZE makes stepped glides on held tones when moved slowly. No clicks, bursts or runaway in any CV case.
9. **HALL's long tails against your Valhalla Room** *(sound; measured)*. A 15 s HALL loses its 2-4 kHz early (T60 0.72 / 0.48 of 1 kHz against the Room's 0.90-0.95 / 0.65-0.77) and has static ringing modes. Moving the loss shelf from 2.5 to 6 kHz and deepening the modulation on long tails bring both close to the Room. 150-520 B. **Page `review2_halltail`.** Also: HALL's and MASSIVE's first 50 ms lean anti-phase (mono −4 to −6 dB), fixable for 0 B.
10. **Robustness is good, with three holes** *(robustness; measured, code)*. 6 hours of fuzzing found no non-finite sample or over-ceiling peak, SHIMMER can't run away (the octave is made from the input, not the tank), and power-up, freezes and switches don't pop. But:
    - one NaN poisons the output forever (the Plugin needs an input guard: 0 B of firmware);
    - **libDaisy refreshes the SDRAM 2.6× slower than the chip's datasheet asks** (code-read; the row count unverified), a margin question for long delay lines;
    - the tightened SDRAM timing has only ever been checked on USB power.

**What's already fine:** the FDN's matrix and DECAY make-up match the theory; late tails are dense, uncorrelated and lose nothing in mono; WEAR's aliasing is low on everything but loud bright drums at full WEAR (fixing it costs more than it's worth); MOTION sits inside real cassette machines' range; SHIMMER's aliasing is solved; the stack uses ~7 KB of 60.

## The three open questions

### MASSIVE's loudness bump just before endless

**In one sentence:** it isn't a bump, it's a cliff. The floor governor switches on within about 3° of the pot at DECAY 0.80-0.81, and how big the step is depends on how hard you play.

**What we do.** Below DECAY 0.85 every setting is linear: play louder and the tail gets louder. At endless, the floor governor holds the tail at a fixed level (−21 dBFS in MASSIVE, ADR 0033) whatever comes in. Across 0.80-0.85 the governor and the ducking fade in (`core/params/SpaceVoicing.h:176`, `core/dsp/Governor.h`). MASSIVE's longest finite tail (120 s at 0.80) is where the most linear setting meets the held one.

**Measured** (agent `bump`, K-weighted, wet against the dry, MASSIVE SIZE 0.8; full table in `review2-notes/bump.md`):

| Playing level | Pad / keys at 0.77-0.80 vs endless | Stabs |
|---|---|---|
| The material's own level | 4.0-5.3 dB louder | 0.5 dB louder |
| 6 dB hotter | 7-9 dB louder | 5.7 dB louder |
| 10 dB quieter | endless is 3-8 dB *louder* | endless 6 dB louder |

- **Where it comes from:** the governor accounts for about 3-3.5 dB, the ducking 0.5-1.2 dB, DECAY's make-up at most 0.6 dB.
- **The defect is the step:** about 75 % of the change happens between 0.80 and 0.81, 2.5-4.6 dB per 0.01 of DECAY. At 20 % weight the governor's added loss is already 5-10 times the tank's own loss at 120 s.
- **What a slow turn sounds like:** from endless to 0.70 over 20 s, the level rises about 4 dB around 3 o'clock and overshoots 0.70's own level by 1.5-2 dB for about 10 s. No click, no swell artefact.
- **PLATE has the same cliff, smaller** (1.7 dB at the material's level, 5.2 dB hot). HALL has none at normal levels; its floor sits 3 dB higher.
- **I re-checked it on different material** (the skank loop, ref-main renderer, wet RMS over 20-40 s), and it reproduces:
  - At the skank's own level (−33 dBFS RMS, quiet), endless is 4.5 dB *louder* than 0.78, rising about 1 dB per 0.01 above 0.80.
  - 12 dB hotter, 0.78 is 4.2 dB louder than endless, with a **3.1 dB step between 0.80 and 0.81**.

**How other reverbs do it** (the owner's own takes, numbers only): there's no rule that longer means louder. Supermassive Sagittarius gets 0.9-2.1 dB *quieter* at 100 % feedback (our direction, a third to a half the size), Andromeda 5-8 dB louder, ValhallaPlate 8-11 dB louder from short to long, ValhallaRoom flat within ±1 dB. None of the takes sits between 73 and 100 %, so they can't show whether a unit has a cliff.

**Is it a problem?** The level difference itself is how endless works: it's a held level, not a linear one, so it will always be quieter than the longest finite setting when you play hard, and louder when you play softly. **The 3° cliff is the problem.** It's what the owner hears as a "bump" on a slow turn.

**Options:**

| Option | Sound change | CPU | Flash | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E1g: the governor fades in from 0.70 on a gentle curve (t³); ducking as built** | audible in MASSIVE at 0.70-0.85 when played at a normal or hot level; **endless and everything ≤ 0.70 bit-identical** (140 cases, ref-main) | ~0 | +56 B (release 112,620 B, 20 B left) | the owner's ears; page `review2_bump` |
| Leave it, and document it | none | 0 | 0 | — |
| A fixed make-up trim (like PLATE/HALL's) | rejected: it flips the cliff upward for quiet playing (+3.8 dB step) | | | |

- **E1g, measured:** the steepest step per 0.01 drops from 2.5-4.6 dB to 0.3-0.9 dB. The tail after playing at 0.75-0.80 starts 1.4-4.9 dB lower and now joins 0.82's tail instead of sitting 5 dB above it. No clicks; brightness within 0.2 dB. It can only add loss, so nothing can grow.
- **Not fixed by any option:** the 2-3 dB between endless and 0.60-0.70. Closing it would change settings the owner asked to keep (ADR 0033's floor, or MASSIVE's make-up).

**The page's version, after phase 2** (agent `bumppage`): PLATE gets the ramp too, plus one extra piece.
- E1g alone left PLATE a dip at 0.80 and a 0.7 dB step back up at 0.81. The cause: PLATE's v1.0.53 long-tail make-up trim lets go exactly there, hidden today by the governor's bigger step.
- **Mode 4** (`IV_BUMP=4:0:0.7:0:3:5`) ramps the governor *and* PLATE's trim on the same t³ curve from 0.70. MASSIVE is bit-identical to E1g; HALL untouched.
- **PLATE's steepest change per 0.01 of DECAY:** pad 1.0 → 0.2 dB, hot pad 2.3 → 0.6, hot keys 2.1 → 0.7.
- **Held at 0.78** (B − A): MASSIVE −2.5 dB (pad) / −3.3 dB (hot); PLATE −0.8 / −1.6 dB.
- **What it doesn't do:** played hot, the level still climbs about 7 dB from endless to 0.70 in both versions, because 0.70 is louder than a held endless. B makes the climb gradual instead of a step at 3 o'clock.
- **Side effects:** PLATE is a touch brighter-balanced at 0.75-0.80 (+0.5-2.4 dB above 4 kHz: its lows come down more), and up to 1.3 dB louder at 0.77-0.81 when played quietly. Tails after playing start 0.5-3.3 dB lower (MASSIVE 1.4-4.9).
- **Gates:** SAME 60/60 with the hook off and on (re-run by me); in 140 held cases only MASSIVE and PLATE at 0.75-0.82 differ; ctest 100 % of 21; default firmware `cmp`-identical.
- **Flash:** `BUMP_EARLY=3` **+64 B (112,628 B, 12 B under budget)**.
- **Docs if adopted:** an ADR 0008 amendment (the governor would act before endless) and the T60 test tables.

### The rumble at endless DECAY with TONE bright

**In one sentence:** the floor governor nudges the tail's level up and down to hold it, but each nudge also tilts the tone (the down nudges take highs, the up nudges add lows), so over minutes the tail drains its highs and piles up its lows. Separately, any DC coming in is kept forever.

**What we do.** At endless DECAY the loop has no loss of its own, so the only correction is the governor's (`Plate.cpp:184`, `Fdn.cpp:394`). Two details make that correction spectral:
- when it adds loss, it switches on the "air" (v1.0.25) in full (`Governor.h:176`: `air > 0 && lowRate > 0`), so a correction of +0.0001 per second already takes −0.29 dB per trip at 1 kHz and −1.8 dB at 5 kHz, against −0.005 dB at DC;
- when it gives level back, the high rate is clamped (`Governor.h:179`), so the make-up is a low shelf below ~2.5 kHz.

The governor dithers around its hold (up to 80 % of its chunks on the loss side). The total level stays put while the spectrum is rectified, and with a DC gain of exactly 1 and no DC blocker in the loop, the bottom end ends up flat per Hz down to ~10 Hz.

**Measured** (agent `rumble`, 10-minute renders as band envelopes; in-loop probes on the Plate):
- **PLATE**, burst, TONE fully CW: above 2 kHz −46 dB, 1-20 Hz +9 dB, 40-200 Hz +9 dB over 10 minutes. **HALL** −37 / +8. **MASSIVE**, whose air is 0, only +4 dB in the lows. The ranking follows each SPACE's air (PLATE 8 dB/s, HALL 5.5, MASSIVE 0), which is why PLATE is worst.
- **The controls rule out the other suspects:** with the hold off, the spectrum keeps (−0.5 dB above 2 kHz in 100 s). MOTION at 0 or 1, modulation off and WEAR 0.5 all leave the drift. Loop peaks are about 0.1, far under the soft clip's knee (0.84), and the Neve's even term is 0 at endless, so **no DC is made inside the loop**. TONE's tilt and bell sit after the loop: TONE CW only makes the fade more obvious.
- **Freezes drain too:** a HALL freeze loses 32 dB above 2 kHz over 10 minutes, PLATE ~19 dB in 100 s, although ADR 0009 means freezes to keep their highs.
- **The second mechanism, DC from the input** (agents `robust`, `litfdn`, `blind`, and my own check): the wet path has no DC blocker, so at endless DECAY any DC that comes in stays and uses up the floor's level.
  - **My check** (ref-main, PLATE DECAY 1): 40 s of the skank loop with 0.02 of DC added, then silence. 10-20 s later the tail is a held offset of 0.091 with the music at **−49 dBFS**, against −21 dBFS without the offset.
  - Even the stimulus's own −73 dBFS offset leaves a 0.021 offset in the tail.
  - On the module the WM8731 codec's ADC high-pass is on (`libDaisy codec_wm8731.cpp:55`), which removes steady DC. Slow offsets, a different codec revision, and **the Plugin, which has nothing**, still get it.
- **The hour-long soak** (agent `robust`) sees the same drift at TONE noon: PLATE's 160-640 Hz falls 23 dB in an hour, below 40 Hz rises 9.6 dB, and the total holds within 1 dB.
- **Why the soak audit missed it:** its hold checks measured total level only, which the governor keeps by design. Also, the air arrived in v1.0.25, after the soak's Core (v1.0.20). It did once note "+8.8 dB … the highs fading and the makeup lifting".

**The fix, behind `IV_RUMBLE_FIX` (off by default; finite DECAY bit-identical with it on):**
- **Bit 1, the governor's correction becomes a flat gain.** DECAY's own loss keeps TONE's ratio, the air and ADR 0008's darkening. Over 10 minutes above 2 kHz stays within ±1 dB (as built −34 to −46), sub-20 Hz rises at most 2.9 dB (as built +6 to +9), total within ±0.8 dB. Audible from about 3 s after a hit: 40-200 Hz 6 dB lower, above 2 kHz 3-5 dB higher. Flash +200 B (measured, lean form).
- **Bit 16, the still-lows tank holds its own level.** Without it, HALL after loud noise still had its lows rising 11 dB in 10 minutes (the lossless low tank was taking the shared make-up). Flash ~100-150 B (estimated).
- **Bit 2, a one-pole DC blocker (3-5 Hz) on the wet's input, outside the loop.** It removes the DC build-up completely. −0.07 to −0.17 dB at 20-40 Hz; ~0.2 % of the CPU budget (estimated); +144 B (measured). It doesn't touch ADR 0008 or the loop.
- **Not recommended:** a DC blocker inside the loop (no DC is made there), or fading the air out at endless on its own (the lows rise 10 dB in 100 s).
- **Flash:** bits 1 + 16 + 2 come to ~450-500 B against 76 B spare. The sound-neutral flash savings in section 2 (−2 to −5 KB) pay for it.

**The final candidate, `IV_RUMBLE_FIX=19` (bits 1 + 2 + 16; agent `endless`):**
- **Why 19 and not 17:** 17 alone does nothing against DC. With 0.01 of DC under the skank in PLATE, at 2 minutes the wet DC is +0.055 and the mids and highs sit at −61 / −70 dBFS with 17; −0.0002 and −20 / −32 dBFS with 19.
- **The DC blocker's corner: 5 Hz.** It removes the same DC as 3 Hz and 3-4 dB more of a 1 Hz bleed. Its whole cost in the music band: 20-40 Hz −0.20 dB, 40-200 Hz −0.03 dB, above that ≤ 0.03 dB. 1-20 Hz is −2.5 dB, which is the point.
- **10-minute endless holds** (3 SPACEs × TONE 0 / 0.5 / 1 × WEAR 0 / 0.5): at TONE fully clockwise, as built loses 17-19 dB above 2 kHz and lifts the lows; the fix keeps every band within ±1 dB.
  - Energy is bounded: per-minute totals within 0.8 dB (1.7 dB as built), peaks ≤ −3.1 dBFS.
  - Freezes over 10 minutes: as built −14 to −23 dB above 2 kHz; the fix within ±0.2 dB.
  - Fed holds (−12, 0, +6 dB and WEAR 1): bounded, peaks ≤ −2.4 dBFS.
  - No clicks or bursts on DECAY, TONE or FREEZE moves.
- **Still true with the fix, and a question for the owner:** at TONE noon and below the designed darkening never stops, in either version. The hold keeps the tail's level, so it never reaches the floor where ADR 0008 meant the darkening to end. Over 10 minutes the mids drain 8-25 dB and the lows gain 2-8 dB; with the fix, MASSIVE at TONE 0 gains a little more lows than as built (+7.6 against +4.4 dB, 1-20 Hz).
  - **Possible next prototype:** the hold makes up only what the modulation loses, not what the darkening takes.
- **Gates:**
  - hook off SAME 60/60 (re-run by me at the branch head);
  - with 17 every finite-DECAY case bit-identical (49/49);
  - ctest 100 % of 21;
  - default firmware `cmp`-identical to v1.0.55 (re-checked by me).
- **Flash, measured:** `ENDLESS_FIX=1` release **113,132 B: +568 B** (`Fdn::control` +236, the blocker +158, memos +48, member offsets +124). Without the blocker +472 B. It needs the flash from section 2 first.
- **Listen:** `renders/review2_endless`.

### MASSIVE's CPU margin

**Why, and what the literature says** (agents `valhalla` and `litfdn`; sources in their notes):
- **HALL's dull upper mids:** the loss shelf is first-order and crosses at 2.5 kHz (`SpaceVoicing.h:112`), so it already shortens 1 kHz; with the highs at 0.45 × the lows' T60 (`:101`) and air at 5.5 dB/s, 2-4 kHz die early. A prototype with the corner at 6 kHz (`IV_LOSS_SHELF_HZ=6000`) reads 1.04 / 0.91 / 0.71 against Dark Chamber's 1.07 / 0.90 / 0.65. This partly reverses the owner's tone_shelf pick (v1.0.15). **On a page: `review2_halltail`.**
- **HALL's ringing modes:** at 15 s its 0.91 s of loop gives ~0.9 modes per Hz. The modes barely overlap (~0.14 where a smooth tail needs ~3), and the modulation floor (×3) is too small to hide them. Every source puts modulation first among the mitigations: Lexicon's RHall "random delays reduce long-lived modes" (480L manual), and Schlecht's time-varying FDNs. `IV_MOD_SCALE=2` takes the spikes from 4.5 ‰ to 1.9 ‰ and the spread to 8-12 cents. **Same page.**
- **PLATE's sparse start:** Costello's plate post, Griesinger 1989, Välimäki 2012 and the 480L manual all describe a plate as dense at once. Ours is Dattorro's own topology, so we match the paper and depart from the hardware. Options: more PLATE input diffusion (CPU 0) or a short velvet-noise onset (1-3 %). The owner called the PLATE "excellent", so it's a question, not a fix. *Not built.*
- **HALL's quiet onset:** its two early taps per line sit at 0.355 with gains from 0.3 (`SpaceVoicing.h:142-147`). Raising them 3-6 dB costs 0 CPU and 0-16 B. A level question; the owner kept the smooth profile on `early_hits`. *Not built.*
- **The anti-phase start:** while one line's first echo is the only thing sounding, L and R carry it with the product of two ±1 signs, so it's in or out of phase line by line (`Fdn.cpp:782-785`). Re-signing the early taps costs 0 CPU and ~0 B. It's a tiny audible change and needs a golden refresh. *Not built; a good candidate for the next session.*
- **MASSIVE hardly moves in pitch:** its modulation floor is absolute while its lines are ~10 × HALL's, so a tail makes far fewer passes a second. MOTION's Wander at 0.9 reaches only 5.7 cents. Scaling the floor with line length costs 0 CPU and ~20 B. That changes MASSIVE's character, so it's the owner's MOTION question; this is the number to give them.
- **What's fine** (measured, `litfdn`):
  - The Hadamard matrix and sign vectors are smoother than 58 % of random ±1 alternatives.
  - The DECAY loudness make-up is Jot's law: exponents 0.47 / 0.445 against the theory's 0.5.
  - HALL's bass multiply is 1.0, inside Lexicon's 1.0-1.5.
  - The in-loop allpasses' loss lumping (Blesser's warning in Dattorro 1997) measures straight decays from 500 Hz up; the exact cure isn't worth it.
  - Late tails are Gaussian-dense in every SPACE.
- **A correction to the last review:** it said nothing states "don't modulate the lows". Schlecht's thesis §6.3.1 (p. 100) does: modulated lows were "easily perceived as detuned", so only content above ~400 Hz was modulated. Our 300 Hz still-lows tank is a direct relative, now with a citation.
- **PLATE's highs outlast an EMT 140's by 2.5-4×** (10 kHz T60 2.65 s for a 4 s plate; Costello: under 1 s on an EMT at every damper setting). That's the owner's calibration, not a fault. Only worth a page if a "more EMT" plate is wanted.

### 1.2 MASSIVE's hole at 250 Hz at SIZE noon (found tonight)

**In one sentence:** at SIZE noon, the default, MASSIVE's two tanks (the main one and the still-lows one) ring almost alike near their 300 Hz crossover, so the crossover's two halves cancel, and the low mids sag 3-6 dB.

- **Found by agent `blind`, confirmed by agent `hole`** across pink noise, pad, keys and a synth bass, DECAY 0.3-1.0, playing and tail, both channels:
  - **The dip:** the summed output against the two tanks' added powers over 200-400 Hz is −2.5 to −6.7 dB at SIZE 0.50, the deepest point in all 32 combinations.
  - **The cause:** the two tanks' coherence at 200-400 Hz is 0.52-0.83 at SIZE 0.50, against 0.08-0.34 at 0.40. They share the Hadamard matrix, the early-tap layout and the allpasses, and their line lengths are equal in seconds to within 0.38 ms. A Butterworth low-pass plus high-pass of one coherent signal notches at the crossover.
  - **A diagnostic hook** (`IV_HOLE_SOLO`) takes the wet from one tank at a time; the two solos sum back to the full wet within −135 dB.
- **Noon isn't the only hole:** SIZE 0.75 (−5.3 dB playing), 0.70 (−3.8), 1.00 (−3.9) and 0.46-0.48 (−1.4 to −4.2); most other SIZEs carry −0.5 to −2.5 dB. **HALL isn't affected** (within ±0.5 dB at every SIZE).
- **It also makes endless MASSIVE quieter than designed:** the governor adds the two tanks' powers rather than measuring their sum, so where they cancel the output settles 1.2-3.5 dB under the floor.
- **The fix, `IV_HOLE_FIX=1:0.94`:** MASSIVE's low-tank lines 6 % shorter.
  - It brings the interference within ±0.8 dB at every SIZE (0-1 in 0.05 steps, 0.45-0.55 in 0.01), HALL and MASSIVE.
  - Hook off: SAME 60/60. Hook on: only the 20 MASSIVE cases and `flips` differ.
  - Bounded in fed and infinite holds; no clicks on SIZE moves.
  - **+24 B** of flash (`make HOLE_FIX=0.94`), no CPU.
  - Audible: while playing at SIZE 0.5, +3.9 to +4.5 dB at 250 Hz (re-measured; the 160 Hz change depends on the channel, +1.4 dB in stereo power). Single bass notes land on different modes (100 Hz −1.6 dB in the draft). The owner's earlier MASSIVE level picks were all heard with the hole in place.
- **The "wiggle" at DECAY 0.65** (1-1.5 dB in every MASSIVE row, found by `bump`) is a different thing: NATURAL's 12-tap swell, whose spacing follows DECAY, reinforcing or thinning held chords by ±0.5-1 dB. It's smooth in BLOOM and on pink noise. It may be what the owner once heard as "the 12 points accentuate the mids". No fix proposed.
- **To hear it:** a level-matched draft pair (not a page) in `renders/review2_hole_draft/` (as built / fixed). This is the strongest candidate for the next page.

### 1.3 WEAR (ERA in the code)

**In one sentence:** WEAR's tape does alias, but only on loud, bright, broadband material near the top of the knob, and the fix that works costs more than it's worth.

Measured on the real Core stages (agent `litnl`; alias = the output minus the same shaper at 8× with brick-wall decimation):
- **Chords at WEAR 1:** alias at −67 dB or lower at every level. The code's "little to fold" comment holds for tonal material.
- **Drum-like bursts at WEAR 1, wet at −24 dBFS RMS:** −32 to −37 dB in HALL and MASSIVE, 5-8 dB worse in PLATE; at −12 dBFS, −20 to −23 dB. At WEAR 0.5 it's 4-15 dB better.
- **The cause:** the tape's +6 dB emphasis and 3.65× drive push 4-11 kHz into the ±1 clamp; a pure cubic can't alias into the band below 12 kHz.
- **The cures compared:**

  | Cure | Alias | CPU | Flash |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ADAA (divide-free / exact) | −7 to +6 dB (wrong tool: its zeros sit at multiples of fs and miss aliases near Nyquist; Vicanek; Parker et al. DAFx-16 Table 1) | +0.14 % / +0.6-0.7 % | +76 / +236 B |
  | 2× oversampling | 14-50 dB lower | +1.45 to +1.8 % | +388 B |

- **Recommendation: change nothing.** Build a hook-only 2× prototype only if the owner hears fizz on hats at WEAR above ~0.8.
- **Running the chain at 24 kHz** (an era-rethink idea) would alias 7-19 dB more. Don't.
- **The tank loop's own saturator at 24 kHz:** −54 to −80 dB per pass on a tail-like spectrum. Not a problem.
- **The AM radio's over-modulation clamp:** −41 to −52 dB after its band-pass. A soft knee (+68 B) isn't worth it.
- **The BBD and compander** match Raffel & Smith 2010, with two deliberate departures:
  - the unfiltered sample-and-hold clock (images −9 to −23 dB: the owner's picked fizz);
  - an expander slower than the compressor (12-32 ms against 10 ms; a real NE570 pair shares one RC), which exaggerates breathing.

  Ours has ~2 % second harmonic (Raffel ~1 % per 1,024 stages) and a third harmonic about a sixth of the second, which the owner picked lower in v1.0.49. Leave it.
- **Gain staging:** not measured inside the Engine tonight. The wets were scaled to −30 to −12 dBFS RMS by hand. *Gap: see section 7.*

### 1.4 MOTION (DRIFT in the code)

**In one sentence:** MOTION's tape side sits at the heavy end of real cassette machines, and its Wander is deliberately far stronger than the classic reverbs'. Both are choices, not faults.

|---|---|---|

### 1.5 SHIMMER, BLOOM, FREEZE / PAINT, the ducking and the floor governor

- **The floor governor** is the source of both the bump (open question 1) and the long-hold drift (open question 2). Its *level* job is right; its *tone* side-effect (the air switching on with any loss) is the defect.
- **Freezes lose their highs over minutes** (measured: HALL −32 dB above 2 kHz in 10 minutes). The same governor mechanism; the same fix.
- **SHIMMER is safe as built, and nothing needs changing** (agent `shimmer`, measured on the desktop; notes `review2-notes/shimmer.md`):
  - **It can't run away, by construction:** the shifter hears the diffused *input*, never the tank, and its octave joins the tank's input (`Engine.cpp:984-1007`, verified), so there's no feedback.
  - Over 3 SPACEs × DECAY 0.80-1.0 × DEPTH 0-1 × input −30 to −6 dBFS, fed level growth matches DEPTH 0 within 0.6 dB. Holds after the input stops slope −0.15 to +0.07 dB/min, the same as without the octave, and there's no pumping against the governor or the ducking.
  - **FREEZE and PAINT can't stack octaves:** the send rests while frozen (`Engine.cpp:634`, verified), and PAINT copies the twin, which hears the raw input. Fifteen freeze → paint → unfreeze cycles over 10 minutes: the painted level drifts ≤ 1.6 dB, the same as NATURAL, and the octave doesn't grow.
  - **Aliasing is solved:** an 8th-order filter with eight zeros at 24 kHz before the shifter. Folds are −88 dB or lower at 12.5 kHz input, −118 dB or lower in the audible range on a sweep; in-band spurs ≤ −75 dB re the octave.
  - **The grain wobble is by design and masked:** random-phase grains wander −11/+5 dB in 10 ms on a held sine (Rayleigh statistics), and the tank's own modulation does the same to the fundamental in NATURAL. Valhalla Shimmer chose the same family on purpose (Costello, *ValhallaShimmer notes*, "a bit of history").
  - **The window approximation doesn't matter to the sound:** Bhaskara's sin² sums to a constant within 0.0005 dB; `SHIMMER_POLY` is purely a CPU choice (+0.011 dB).
  - **No clicks of its own** on DEPTH, DECAY, freeze or paint moves.
  - **A question for the owner's ears, not a fault: in an endless hold, the octave mellows away over 2-4 minutes** (PLATE at −18 dBFS: +3.8 dB of octave → −0.1 dB; at −6 dBFS +11 → −1). That's ADR 0008's designed darkening at work: an endless SHIMMER pad ends where an endless NATURAL pad ends. Keep it, or should the sparkle hold? (Note the endless fix's flat correction changes how fast this happens; listen on `review2_endless`.)
  - **For "Shimmer inside the reverb"** (the TASKS item): Dal Santo, Pi, Prawda, Schlecht and Välimäki, "Shimmer Reverberation with Nonlinear Feedback Delay Networks", DAFx26 (§4.1, eqs. 7-8): each in-loop shifter is followed by a 76 Hz DC blocker and an adaptive gain tracking x²/y² over 50 ms; shifters on 4 of 8 lines. At our endless DECAY an in-loop octave would climb pass after pass; the governor caps level, not the climb. It would cost +10-19 % CPU per shifter (estimated). After v1.0.
  - **Seen on the way, not SHIMMER's:** PLATE SIZE jumps put +26 to +28 dB above 3 kHz into the following second on a pure-tone chord (section 3, finding 4), and MASSIVE's unfreeze measures +22 to +27 dB in its worst 1 ms frame in NATURAL too. *Worth a listen; not investigated.*
- **BLOOM, the twin room, the ducking:** no new finding beyond the bump. The 6 hours of fuzz (section 4) included every ONSET and freeze state without a non-finite sample or a peak over the ceiling.

## 3. Controls and CV

**In one sentence:** nothing clicks or runs away under any CV, but **SIZE and DEPTH ignore CV that moves faster than about 6 % of the travel a second**, short triggers can slip past the gate, and PLATE's SIZE steps when it's moved slowly.

The knob path was **ported, not modelled**, into the renderer behind `IV_KNOBPATH=1` (agent `knobs`; hook off: SAME 60/60):
- **Ported** from `firmware/main.cpp:2676-2717`:
  - one reading per 1 ms block;
  - the 10 ms one-pole, seeded with the first reading;
  - `ivpot::endStops` (the firmware's own header);
  - deadband 0.001, and 0.003 for SIZE and TIME;
  - the gate polled once a block, with no latch;
  - the button through libDaisy's debounce (8 × 1 ms).
- **Modelled:**
  - the value turned back into a raw reading (0.025 + 0.95v, clipped);
  - optional ADC noise and 16-bit quantisation;
  - the ADC's 32× oversampling as a point sample.
- **Not modelled:** audio coupling into the readings, any RC filter on the CV inputs, callback jitter.

Findings (measured unless marked):
1. **SIZE (HALL, MASSIVE) and DEPTH follow only slow CV.** A change reaches the Engine in 0.003 steps, and a crossfade starts only after 50 ms without a new step (ADR 0025). So the tank follows only CV slower than 0.003 per 50 ms, that is **0.06 of the travel per second** (0.3 V/s if 0-5 V spans the knob, unverified).

   SIZE crossfades started in 14 s:

   | CV | HALL | MASSIVE |
   |---|---|---|
   | LFO at 5, 20, 100 or 440 Hz | 0 | 0 |
   | 0.1 Hz LFO, ±0.25 | 24 | 24 |
   | random step every 125 ms | 54 | 76 |

   **What a player hears:** under a fast LFO the room holds the SIZE it had, then jumps when the CV stops; a slow LFO is caught only near its turning points; a sequencer works. ADR 0025's amendment calls this "by design; the owner may revisit", but nothing a CV user reads says so.
2. **No clicks, bursts or runaway** in any case: SIZE, DEPTH, DECAY, TONE, WEAR and MOTION in each SPACE under LFOs, audio-rate CV, steps and slow triangles. Worst level over still settings +3.0 dB on pink noise; HF at most +1.8 dB.
3. **Audio-rate CV aliases:** knobs are read once per 1 ms, so CV above 500 Hz folds (1 kHz reads as a constant offset).
4. **PLATE's SIZE, moved by CV or a slow turn, makes stepped glides on held tones:** energy above 3 kHz is +14.8 dB over a still knob through the knob path, against +7.8 dB through the renderer's smooth automation; pink noise shows 0 dB.
   - The 48-sample block accounts for +4.5 dB and the deadband for +2.5 dB.
   - **The renderer under-reports this:** its automation runs 16-sample micro-blocks.
   - A fix (ramping the PLATE's taps per sample) costs ~0.3-0.6 % and ~100-200 B (estimated). It's the "PLATE's SIZE by crossfade" item already on the owner's list.
5. **The gate misses triggers shorter than about 1 ms:** it's polled once per block with no latch (`main.cpp:2683`, verified). A 0.5 ms trigger 10 samples into a block renders bit-identical to no gate at all, in all three SPACEs. Latching edges between blocks costs ~30-60 B (estimated) and needs the module.
6. **Fast gates and taps behave as designed:** 2-50 Hz gates and 10 Hz taps toggle the freeze at half the rate; a gate held through SPACE changes holds the flip until the unfreeze; painting while SIZE moves is level.
7. **A noisy pot is fine:** with 4-66 LSB of noise (66 is twice the rack's measured ±0.0005), SIZE and TIME never send after the first reading, and the other knobs stay within 0.2 dB. The unknown is audio coupling into the readings, the rack's real noise source.
8. **libDaisy's "2 ms" knob slew does nothing at a 1 kHz control rate:** its coefficient is 1/(0.002 · 1000 · 0.5) = 1.0, a pass-through (`libDaisy/src/hid/ctrl.cpp:30`, verified). The comment at `main.cpp:2695` ("on top of libDaisy's 2 ms") is wrong; the 10 ms one-pole is the only smoothing.

**What only the module can answer:** the CV range in volts (whether negative CV subtracts, where pot + CV clips: the SPEC says "0-5 V + pot offset", and libDaisy's Versio file gives only the ADC scaling), any RC filter on the CV inputs, callback jitter against 1 ms triggers, the CPU of continuous SIZE crossfades under CV, and audio coupling into the knob readings.

## 4. Robustness

**In one sentence:** the Core never produced a non-finite value or an over-ceiling sample in 6 hours of fuzzing. The real issues are on the input side: DC is kept forever at endless, and one NaN poisons the Plugin's output until it's reloaded.

Agent `robust`, a dedicated harness (`tools/review2/robust.cpp` on its branch); measured on the desktop unless marked:
1. **The long-hold drift** (open question 2): PLATE endless at TONE noon loses 23 dB in 160-640 Hz over an hour while the total holds within 1 dB. **Not a desktop artefact:** the same run with module-like arithmetic (no fused multiply-adds) tracks within 0.1 dB.
2. **DC at the input:** 0.1 of DC at endless pushes the music down 19 dB in PLATE, 16.5 in HALL, 14 in MASSIVE. A 1 Hz sine at 0.2 costs MASSIVE 15.8 dB. `Filters.h`'s `DcBlocker` is dead code. The fix is bit 2 of the endless fix.
3. **Hot Eurorack input:**
   - The Versio clips at about 16 Vpp, so 0 dBFS is about ±8 V (the NE Desmodus manual, via Resilio's notes), and the Core never sees more than ±1.
   - The wet never exceeded 0.891 in 216 cases, and the limiter never acts on clipped music.
   - **At mid BLEND a full-scale square reaches 1.17 after the output trim and the DAC hard-clips** (`f2s24` clamps to ±1, no wrap). That's 13 % of samples on a full-scale square, ≤ 0.5 % on noise, and 0 on music.
   - An output soft clip would cost 40-80 B. Low priority.
4. **One NaN, Inf or sample ≥ 1e25 poisons the output forever,** in every SPACE, ONSET and freeze state. In PLATE frozen with a 1e30 spike the tank stays finite while the output is NaN, so the last review's proposed guard on the governors' power would miss it. The likely path is the input-power follower (inference). **The module can't get these from its ADC.** The Plugin can (a host bug, a bad upstream plugin).
   - **Recommendation:** sanitise the input in the Plugin's `processBlock` (non-finite → 0, clamp ±8): 0 B of firmware.
   - `IV_INPUT_GUARD` (desktop prototype) recovers every case.
5. **Fuzz, 6 hours, clean:**
   - knob jumps every block; SPACE, ONSET, button and gate at random;
   - blocks of 1-256 samples;
   - inputs: full-scale Nyquist, DC, square, noise, clicks.

   No non-finite sample; tank peak +0.1 dBFS.
6. **Power-up, freezes, switches: no pop or burst.**
   - The wet's early peak is ≤ 1.06× its later peak over 60 random power-up knob sets.
   - SPACE flips, freezes and ONSET switches on a music bed step at 1.10-1.23×.
   - BLEND starts at its 0.5 default and glides to the knob in ~5 ms, a tiny dry blip (fix 20-40 B; not worth it now).
7. **Desktop against module maths: a correction to the premise.** **Release does not set flush-to-zero**; only the profile build does. There's no `vmsr fpscr` in the release disassembly, `main.cpp:1955` says "as release runs (no FZ)", and ADR 0026 rejected it after round 11 measured subnormals cost nothing.
   - Release has no fused multiply-adds in the Core (the 62 `vfma` are in newlib and one libDaisy routine).
   - The desktop's `libiv_core.a` has 901.
   - FMA against no-FMA on the desktop: 60 of 60 renders differ, by −100 to −54 dBFS per 12 s render.
   - **What that means for every claim:** `same_render`'s SAME n/n proves desktop against desktop only. Soak and drift results carry over as statistics, not waveforms (the no-FMA soak tracked within 0.1 dB). Subnormals agree between the renderer and the module (neither flushes); the Plugin flushes (`ScopedNoDenormals`), which differs only below 1e-37.
   - **The cheap match:** `target_compile_options(iv_core PRIVATE -ffp-contract=off)`. That build passes ctest without a golden refresh. What's left is libm differences in control-rate calls and the Plugin's FZ.
8. **The tightened SDRAM timing (3/3 against libDaisy's 16/10):**
   - The SDRAM clock is 100 MHz. The chip (AS4C16M32MSA-6BIN, industrial −40 to 85 °C) needs tRP = tRCD = 18 ns, so 3 clocks = 30 ns is a 67 % margin (datasheet text not extracted: unverified).
   - libDaisy's 16/10 are untuned placeholders ("started at 2" in its source).
   - These waits are counted in the FMC's own clock, so rack power and temperature shouldn't shift them much (estimate).
   - `SDRAMCHK` was clean in all eight profile runs, all on USB power.
   - **The contradiction:** `firmware/README.md:141` says "**The release build never changes the timing**", against `Makefile:30` (`RELEASE_SDRAM_TIGHT ?= 1`) and the README's own lines 172 and 178. (Verified.)
   - **The risk, weighed:** low, but untested where it matters. **To close it:** a build that runs the SDRAM check continuously on rack power, warm, and shows the result on an LED.
9. **A bigger SDRAM item nobody has looked at: the refresh rate.**
   - libDaisy programs `HAL_SDRAM_ProgramRefreshRate(0x81A − 20)` (`libs/libDaisy/src/dev/sdram.cpp:158`, verified): 2,054 SDRAM clocks, ~20.5 µs between refresh commands.
   - If the chip has 8,192 rows per 64 ms, it needs one every ~7.8 µs, so a full pass takes **168 ms against the 64 ms the datasheet guarantees, 2.6× too slow** (the row count is the agent's, unverified).
   - Rows the tank touches are refreshed by the access itself. But a delay line longer than 64 ms holds written data unread for its length, and MASSIVE's lines are long.
   - At room temperature real DRAM retention is usually far longer than 64 ms, so this is a margin question, not a likely fault.
   - The fix is one constant, ~8-16 B and ~0.5 % more of the SDRAM's time. It needs a module check.
10. **Pot noise against the deadband:** the 10 ms one-pole cuts uncorrelated noise ~13 dB, so still-knob jitter of ≤ 0.001 raw becomes ~0.0002 peak-to-peak: 5× margin under the 0.001 deadband, 15× under 0.003.
