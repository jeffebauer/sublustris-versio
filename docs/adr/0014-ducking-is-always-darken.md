# 0014 — Ducking is always DARKEN, growing with DECAY

**Status:** Superseded, 2 Nov 2026 (v1.0.61, ADR 0008's amendment of that date: endless follows the playing, so there's no build-up to hold back, and the duck is gone). Accepted, 21 Oct 2026 (owner, after listening). Spec-session decisions 16, 24, 27. Supersedes the draft's DUCK switch (Darken / Push back / Level, after the brief's Warble was swapped for Level).

**Context:** The brief borrows the concept of ducking past a feedback threshold from Desmodus (not its "scream"): past ~3 o'clock DECAY goes infinite and the tail should get out of the way while you play. The draft gave the ducking its own switch. The owner wasn't sure that was the best use of a switch and asked to hear each style. In the demos (`renders/p7_sw1_demos/`, sketch in `docs/prototypes/p7-sw1-demos/`) the owner picked **DARKEN on both materials** (dub skank and vibes).

**Decision:**
- Ducking has **no switch: it is always DARKEN.** While you play, a loop low-pass closes (down to ~1.5 kHz at full depth) and the tail dips ~3 dB; when you stop, it brightens back.
- **Depth grows with DECAY:** 0 below DECAY 0.85, rising to full at DECAY 1.
- Envelope follower on the input: attack 5 ms, release 400 ms, −42 dBFS → no duck, −24 dBFS → full (starting guesses, from the demos).
- SW1 is freed for ATTACK (ADR 0015).

**Rejected (heard in the demos):** PUSH BACK (more smear, narrower image), LEVEL (plain dip), and a switch to choose between them; a duck depth fixed once infinite.

**Amendment (21 Oct 2026, M7 build; SPEC v1.0.4):** DARKEN's low-pass sits on the wet output, not inside the loop. A filter in the loop removes the highs from the tail for good, so the tail could never "brighten back when you stop"; on the output it does (measured: highs above 4 kHz 4 % of the wet while playing at infinite, 31 % a second after). There's no ducking while frozen: playing over a pad mustn't darken the pad.

**Amendment (23 Oct 2026, owner's M1 notes and pick on `renders/ducking`; SPEC v1.0.17):** ducking is now **4:1 compression of the wet**, not DARKEN. The owner heard DARKEN as "too extreme", holding "too long", with the held tail jumping up when the input stopped, and asked for something "closer to 4:1 compression rather than extreme ducking, so it feels cohesive" (the usual studio practice: a compressor on the reverb return keyed by the dry). Five versions were compared at infinite DECAY on six materials in all three SPACEs; D (attack 30 ms, release 0.4 s, 4:1 above −24 dBFS of input, no darkening) won all 18 cards. Still no switch, still only from Decay 0.85 up and not while frozen. The title stands for the history; the decision is the amendment.


**Amendment (28 Oct 2026, owner's picks on `renders/level_duck` and `renders/level_glue`; SPEC v1.0.51):** ducking is now **2:1**, and it **follows the playing**.
- **Why 2:1:** after the v1.0.49 rack test the owner wrote "ducking on large washy sounds still feels a bit heavy handed, I think we can reduce it from 4:1 to 2:1 or even 1.5:1". Measured (`docs/research/level-at-infinite.md`): at infinite DECAY 4:1 took 5.4 dB on average while playing (a held washy chord 6.8 dB), 2:1 takes 3.6. On `level_duck` the owner picked D, 2:1 with the governor's floor at −21 dBFS (ADR 0033): "Duck 2:1 floor21 sounds the best".
- **Why it follows the playing:** on D the owner still heard "an audible dip once the dry signal stops" and asked to "glue the dry and wet at points of transition so they sound more integrated rather than two separate layers". While a chord is held, the duck kept the wet ~3 dB under the level its tail settles at; at the stop the 0.4 s release brought it back over about a second, so the 50:50 sum dipped (up to 2.3 dB for 0.5 s, MASSIVE stop-start) and then swelled back. `level_glue` compared a faster release (B), a release that depends on the playing (C), less ducking on held playing (D′) and C + D′ (E); the owner picked **E on every card**.
- **What E does:** the input counts as *held* when its ~10 ms power is within 3 dB of its 0.5 s power, judged only while it is over the threshold and smoothed over 0.3 s. Held playing gets 40 % of the reduction (`voicing::kDuckSustainCut` 0.6) and is let go in 0.08 s (`kDuckSustainReleaseSeconds`); short sounds (stabs) keep the full 2:1 and the 0.4 s release, so nothing pumps (the stabs' level spread 5.7 vs 5.5 dB HALL, 9.0 vs 9.0 MASSIVE). Measured on the page: the stop's dip 0.6-1.0 dB and never more than 1 dB under for any length of time (was 0.6-2.3 dB for up to 0.47 s), the wet under held playing within ~1 dB of its tail (was 1.7-3.5 dB under).
- **Established practice:** a program-dependent release judged on the signal's crest factor is the standard automatic-release design for compressors (Giannoulis, Massberg and Reiss, JAES 2012 and 2013); ducked delays and reverbs on hardware expose ratio, threshold and a short release for the same reason (Eventide TimeFactor's DuckedDelay, the TC 2290's dynamic delay, ValhallaDelay's ducking). Sources in `docs/research/level-at-infinite.md`.
- Still no switch, still only from DECAY 0.85 up and not while frozen.
