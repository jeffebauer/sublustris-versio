About your question on the last page ("A sounds like it's going through a more resonant filter"): it is. In today's version, each tiny correction that holds the endless tail's level also switches on the "air" (the extra top-end loss that makes short tails sound like a room), pass after pass. That works like a very steep filter on the top end that keeps closing over the minutes, so the highest notes still left ring out at its edge, like a resonant low-pass slowly sweeping down. Taking just that air out of today's version makes it sound like the fix. B and C don't do it.

At TONE noon the darkening that's already in the design (the highs fade at an endless DECAY when TONE is at noon or below) is stronger than the light settle, so B and C sound the same there.

Card 5 is the one place where C might not be the one. On bright material the light settle takes the top down, and the part that keeps the tail at a steady level makes up for it by slowly bringing the low end up underneath, so after a minute or two C's tail is darker and fuller in the bass than B's (B stays as it was; today's A drains its top much further and its bass comes up too). Listen to the low end under the settled top at 5 and 10 minutes, C against B.
