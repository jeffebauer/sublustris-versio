Is the drop at DECAY's 3 o'clock crossing in the reverb, or in the blend? (owner, 1 Nov 2026: "how much the dry/wet balance and curve is impacting my perception"; docs/research/wet-dry-balance.md). Today's sound (v1.0.60), HALL, SIZE 0.9. Every file is the whole output, dry and wet together, at the BLEND its version names, so there's no MIX fader on this page. Not level-matched: the levels are the point. The trace under each card shows the level of the wet alone (bold), each full mix, and the dry alone.

Every card follows the same timeline (the chapter bar):
  0 s    playing, DECAY just under 3 o'clock (0.78)
  8 s    ↑ into endless: DECAY fully clockwise in 0.5 s
  16 s   ↓ out to 0.78
  24 s   ↑ into endless again
  30 s   ↓ out to 0.7 (about 2 o'clock)
  36 s   stop playing: the dry leaves, the tail rings on at 0.7

Rows: the pad, the keys and plucked hits, each played quietly and hot. The last row is the pad played hot with the crossing paced over 3 s (C on renders/decay_cross), at the same four BLENDs, so you can hear the two effects apart.

A_blend100   BLEND fully clockwise: the wet alone
B_blend75    BLEND at 75 % (about 2 o'clock)
C_blend50    BLEND at noon: an even, equal-power blend (as you play it at the rack)
D_blend30    BLEND at 30 % (about 10 o'clock)

What to listen for: the wet makes the same move at every BLEND; how big it sounds depends on how much dry sits over it. While you play, the dry covers much of the wet's drop; when the playing stops, the dry leaving is a drop of its own.

What we measured (each turn against the same render with DECAY left where it was, so the material's own ups and downs cancel out; the mean over 1-3 s after the turn):
- The step is in the reverb, and the wet makes the same move at every BLEND. Played hot, turning into endless pulls the wet down 4-5.6 dB (up to 8 dB at its deepest). Played quietly, endless is louder: up 3.5-4 dB; turning back out drops it 3.5-5 dB.
- The blend sets how much of that reaches the whole output. Sustained, hot playing hides most of it: the mix moves 0.9-1.4 dB at noon, 0.2-0.5 dB at 30 %. But at noon the balance between dry and wet still changes by the wet's full 4-5 dB, and noon is where listeners hear that balance change best (2-3 dB is enough).
- Sparse or quiet material hides less: with gaps between the plucked hits, the mix at noon still moves 80-90 % as much as the wet turning out of endless (60-85 % turning in); quiet pad and keys, 70-90 % turning out, about half to two thirds turning in.
- Stopping playing is a drop of its own: at noon the whole output falls 6.5-8 dB when the dry leaves, against about 5 dB in the wet alone; at 30 %, 7.5-11 dB. That drop can sound like the reverb dropping.
