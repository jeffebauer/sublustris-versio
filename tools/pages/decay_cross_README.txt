Turning DECAY across 3 o'clock, into endless and back out (owner, v1.0.60: "I was in HALL with decay up to massive, and size large. The reverb swelled into a tail, and I then dropped the decay below the 3 o'clock threshold into endless decay territory, and there was a very quick drop off in tail and amplitude that felt jarring"). HALL, SIZE 0.9, every source played hot (a rack level). Renders are 100 % wet; the MIX fader starts at noon (an even, equal-power blend, as BLEND at noon on the module) and blends the dry in live. Not level-matched on purpose: the level is what this page is about, and every version is bit-identical to A until the knob first moves.

Every card follows the same timeline (the chapter bar):
  0 s    playing, DECAY just under 3 o'clock (0.78): the tail swells
  8 s    ↑ into endless: DECAY turned fully clockwise in 0.5 s
  16 s   ↓ out of endless: DECAY back to about 2:30 (0.75) in 0.5 s
  24 s   stop playing: the tail rings on at 0.75
  27 s   ↑ into endless (tail): the same turn up, on the tail alone
  35 s   ↓ out (tail): and back down; the tail then fades out

Why it drops today: past about 3 o'clock the tail is endless, and a governor holds it at a steady level (about −18 dBFS in HALL) so it never grows. A tail that swelled above that level gets pulled down to it as soon as you turn into endless: 5-7 dB within half a second to a second. Turning back out, a 2 dB level trim snaps back within 40 ms and the tail goes from holding to fading at once. The knob crosses the whole 3 o'clock zone in a few hundredths of a second.

B, C, D let DECAY cross that zone at its own pace, whichever way you turn it, so the tail eases down to the endless level, or eases from holding into its new length. Turning anywhere else (within the finite range, or within endless) is exactly as today, bit for bit.
