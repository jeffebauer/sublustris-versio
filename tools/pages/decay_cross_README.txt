Turning DECAY across 3 o'clock, into endless and back out (owner, v1.0.60: "I was in HALL with decay up to massive, and size large. The reverb swelled into a tail, and I then dropped the decay below the 3 o'clock threshold into endless decay territory, and there was a very quick drop off in tail and amplitude that felt jarring"). HALL, SIZE 0.9, a pad played hot (+6 dB). Renders are 100 % wet; the MIX fader blends the dry back in. Not level-matched on purpose: the level is what this page is about, and every version is bit-identical to A until the knob moves.

Why it drops: past about 3 o'clock (DECAY 0.85) the tail is endless, and a governor holds it at a steady level (about −18 dBFS in HALL) so it never grows. A tail that swelled above that level while DECAY was long gets pulled down to it as soon as you turn into endless: 5-7 dB within half a second to a second. Turning back out, a 2 dB level trim on the long finite tails snapped in within 40 ms, and the tail went from holding to fading at once. The knob crosses the whole 3 o'clock zone (0.80-0.85) in a few hundredths of a second.

B, C, D let DECAY cross that zone at its own pace, whichever way you turn it, so the tail eases down to the endless level, or eases from holding into its new length. Everything that changes across the zone (the governor, the ducking, the trim, the air's dulling) moves with it. Turning anywhere else, within the finite range or within endless, is exactly as today, bit for bit.
